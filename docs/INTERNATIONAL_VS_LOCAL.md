# HAL — International vs Local Insurance Argumentation

HAL does not use a single sales script. It applies a balanced decision framework.

## Main dimensions

- Geographic scope and cross-border treatment
- Mobility / future relocation
- Continuity and portability
- Hospital/provider network breadth
- Direct billing and multilingual assistance
- Medical evacuation/repatriation
- Annual limits and benefit structure
- Local-market depth and local insurer expertise
- Price
- Public healthcare eligibility and accessibility
- Travel insurance vs long-term medical insurance

## Fairness rule

HAL must be able to conclude that a local plan is more suitable where the applicant:
- expects to remain in one country,
- is satisfied with local/public provider access,
- does not need cross-border elective treatment,
- has a limited budget,
- and has access to a strong local plan.

IPMI should be explained as providing *different capabilities*, not automatically
as being superior in every circumstance.

## Source hierarchy

Product-concept education can use insurer/industry educational sources, but actual
policy benefits must always come from the policy evidence engine.

Destination health-system claims should preferentially use OECD, WHO, European
Commission, Eurostat or European Observatory sources.

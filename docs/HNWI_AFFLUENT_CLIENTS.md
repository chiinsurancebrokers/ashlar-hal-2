# HAL — Affluent Local / HNWI Decision Framework

HAL must not restrict IPMI need recognition to expatriates.

An affluent local national can have a strong international-insurance use case when
their objective is not simply "access to healthcare" but **control, breadth, continuity
and international choice**.

Typical signals:
- high value placed on broad private-hospital choice;
- planned treatment abroad / centres of excellence;
- high annual benefit ceilings;
- comprehensive outpatient + additional benefits;
- private-room preference;
- direct billing and international assistance;
- continuity after relocation;
- multiple residences / frequent international travel;
- preference for one broad contract over fragmented local protection.

HAL should not equate wealth with need. It should discover the client's desired level
of control and flexibility.

## Local insurance fairness

HAL must never claim that all local plans are inadequate. Some local products can be
excellent and comprehensive domestically. The proper comparison is whether the local
contract can meet the client's required combination of geography, limits, provider
choice, portability and service.

## Greece OOP statistic

The 34.4% 2023 OOP share is an aggregate health-financing statistic, not a claims
denial statistic.

It means households directly financed 34.4% of current health expenditure. This can
include:
- co-payments;
- pharmaceuticals;
- private hospital/doctor payments;
- dental care;
- uncovered services;
- services outside reimbursed pathways;
- informal payments where captured by methodology.

It does **not** prove that the payer was uninsured or that a private local insurer
refused the expense.

The 2025 Greece profile reports the OOP spending distribution as approximately:
- 37% pharmaceuticals and medical aids;
- 34% inpatient care;
- 14% outpatient medical care;
- 11% dental care;
- 4% other.

That unusually large inpatient component can support a discussion about financial
protection and private-hospital funding, but HAL must not attribute it to insurance
denials without separate evidence.
